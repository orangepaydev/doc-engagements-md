# Johor Findings Presentation — Deck Outline

Outline for two decks for the meeting with our MDEC contact, who is also involved with Johor state IT. Agreed
2026-10-22. The decks are built from this outline in the same folder.

- **Deck A** ([deck-a-johor-findings.html](deck-a-johor-findings.html)): findings that the contact can take to
  **Johor state leadership**, framed as Johor's own roadmap on the data lake with MDEC as the federal enabler. It
  shows headlines only and stays neutral, with no pitch. It is marked "Prepared by OrangePay".
- **Deck B** ([deck-b-how-we-help.html](deck-b-how-we-help.html)): how we can help shape, define and deliver the
  work, with Hyalo as our delivery approach. Only we show it, in the meeting. It stays with us.
- PDFs: run [export-pdf.sh](export-pdf.sh). Both decks share the Hyalo government deck's visual style
  (`portal-process-designer-node-v3/slides/1-DesignerValueProposition/promt-for-slides-v2-gov.html`).

## Decisions behind the outline

- **Little detail.** Deck A gives directions, not recommendations. These stay internal: the numbers register, the
  delivery-model case studies, effort and time estimates, the sequencing chart, the full longlist, failures by name,
  and OrangePay's payments and credit fit.
- **The Hyalo claim, stated accurately.** "You focus on the definition and the governance; the build won't be the
  bottleneck, and changing direction won't mean starting again." Hyalo makes the build agile. It does not make
  governance agile: the data-sharing framework, getting agreement across 16 PBTs, master data and reorganising
  departments are still the client's work.
- **No other Malaysian project is named or criticised.** The "locked-in definition" risk is shown with China
  evidence (idle turnkey packages; technology on top of unchanged processes).
- **Hyalo product claims follow the government deck's rules.**
  - AI proposes and a responsible officer approves.
  - "AI drafts the complete solution" is badged In progress.
  - Working with legacy systems is "designed to work with", not claimed as working.
  - The board's first questions (hosting, security, continuity) are answered with "Proposed" starting positions.
- **Defaults taken.**
  - Format: HTML and PDF.
  - Deck A is "Prepared by OrangePay".
  - The first step on Deck B is a placeholder.
  - No payments content.

## Deck A: Findings for Johor state leadership (13 slides)

| # | Slide title | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | From data lake to better services | Title. Early findings for discussion |
| 2 | Three pressures make this the right time to act | The data-centre boom (42 approved projects, RM164bn, Q2 2025), the JS-SEZ (Jan 2025), and the state data lake being built |
| 3 | The data lake is a foundation. The question is what to build on it | Three goals: less paperwork, faster processing, new services and investment |
| 4 | We studied five Chinese regions that moved from paper to data-driven government | Zhejiang, Shanghai, Shenzhen, Suzhou, Beijing. Results are mostly self-reported, so we treat them as direction |
| 5 | China went in seven stages, and the order matters | Staircase diagram. Stages 1–3 are the foundations everything else depends on |
| 6 | Johor has already started on several stages | Each of the seven stages mapped to Johor today (OSC 3.0 Plus, command centres, the data lake, IMFC-J) |
| 7 | What worked in China, and what didn't | Five pairs: process before technology, one front door, funded operations, measured outcomes, purpose-limited data |
| 8 | Borrow China's sequence, not its enforcement | What transfers, set against Johor's realities (federal/state/16 PBTs, DSA 2025 federal-only, physical counters) |
| 9 | Opportunities fall into three buckets | Three examples each: quick wins, foundations, strategic |
| 10 | The critical path is a state data-sharing framework | Without it, the data lake is just storage |
| 11 | Six principles for how to deliver it | Government owns and an operator runs; shared services for councils; fund running costs; measure use; write reforms into state rules; choose operators competitively |
| 12 | Questions to answer next | Data lake contents and owner, data-sharing directive, pilot councils, Master Plan 2030, IMFC-J tracking, appetite for sharing data |
| 13 | A short validation workshop is the next step | Confirm the data lake, pick priorities, agree pilots and a baseline |
