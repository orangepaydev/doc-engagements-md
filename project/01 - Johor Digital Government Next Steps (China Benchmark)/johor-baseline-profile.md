# Johor Municipal Governance — Baseline Profile

Baseline for comparing Johor's municipal digital journey with China's centralised, top-down one. Sections 1–5 are
research from another agent, pasted in by the project owner on 2026-10-17. Its claims have **not been
independently verified**. Section 6 adds web-sourced facts and corrections gathered for this project.

---

## 1. Institutional governance structure: distributed vs. centralised

The main difference from a Chinese municipality (Hangzhou, Shenzhen, Suzhou) is how power is spread out and how
decisions get agreed.

```
                       FEDERAL GOV (Putrajaya)
                 [MDEC, Digital Ministry, NRECC]
                                │
                        STATE GOV (Johor)
           [Chief Minister, JPBD / PLANMalaysia Johor]
                                │
    ┌───────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┐
    ▼                           ▼                           ▼
City Councils              Municipal Councils         District Councils
(MBJB, MBIP, MBPG)          (MP Kulai, MP Muar)        (MD Mersing, MD Pontian)
 [Highly Urbanized]          [Semi-Urbanized]             [Rural / Agri]
```

- **Multi-layered federalism.** A Chinese municipal government sits under vertical line-agency control (条条专制)
  and has strong executive authority to mandate city-wide platform adoption. In Johor, power is split three ways:
  the **Federal Government** (policy and digital frameworks such as MDEC and the Digital Ministry), the **State
  Government** (land, water, local planning), and the individual **Local Authorities (PBTs, *Pihak Berkuasa
  Tempatan*)**.
- **Local consensus and socialisation.** A state-wide initiative cannot simply be dictated from above. Council
  Presidents (*Yang DiPertua*) and Mayors (*Datuk Bandar*) control their own municipal budgets and bylaws.
  State-wide platforms need consensus, inter-agency alignment and local political buy-in.

## 2. Demographic and socio-economic stratification

| Segment | Demographics | Digital profile | Service expectations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Urban / tech-savvy youth** (Iskandar Malaysia, JB, Kulai) | Gen-Z and Millennials, often cross-border commuters to Singapore or data-hub workers | High literacy, mobile-first, expects seamless e-services and cashless payment | App-based reporting (e-complaints), online tax payment, paperless permits |
| **Traditional / older** (Muar, Batu Pahat, Segamat, Mersing) | Older adults, lower formal education, agriculture, retail, manual trades | Low literacy, relies on physical transactions, sceptical of digital-only | Physical counters (*kaunter fizikal*), cash options, face-to-face engagement |

Implications:
- **Two-speed citizens.** Councils can't close physical counters without shutting out older voters. Digital
  transformation needs **hybrid workflows**: a digital core with an analogue fallback.
- **Language.** Portals must work in Bahasa Melayu, Mandarin, Tamil and English.

## 3. The data-centre catalyst

Spill-over demand from Singapore and available land and power have made Johor (Sedenak, Kempas, Iskandar Puteri)
Southeast Asia's fastest-growing data-centre hub.

- **Direct impacts:** demand for high-tier tech talent; ancillary jobs (construction, HVAC); rising land values and
  rezoning.
- **Municipal challenges:** pressure on grid and water; a gap between high-tech and local skills; approval
  bottlenecks.
- **Spatial disparity:** growth is concentrated in Southern Johor. Northern and east-coast councils (Mersing, Kota
  Tinggi, Segamat) remain largely agrarian, which risks an intra-state divide.
- **Response:** the state created the *Johor State Data Centre Development Planning Guidelines* to standardise
  council procedures.

## 4. Local-authority landscape (16 PBTs, three tiers)

| Tier | Examples | Capacity | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| City Councils (*Majlis Bandaraya*) | MBJB, MBIP, MBPG | High revenue, dedicated IT divisions, smart-city command centres (CCTV, GIS, parking apps) | Direct recipients of tier-1 / hyperscale DC investment |
| Municipal Councils (*Majlis Perbandaran*) | Kulai, Muar, Batu Pahat, Kluang | Moderate budgets; Kulai is forced into rapid digitisation by Sedenak Tech Park | Mixed urban core and rural fringe |
| District Councils (*Majlis Daerah*) | Mersing, Pontian, Kota Tinggi, Tangkak, Segamat | Low assessment base, dependent on state/federal subsidy, minimal in-house engineering | Older, agricultural, low digital adoption |

## 5. Comparative framework: Johor vs. China

| Dimension | Johor | China |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Executive governance | Federated, decentralised | Top-down, centralised |
| Platform architecture | Fragmented, patchwork systems | Unified "City Brain" platform |
| Socialisation strategy | Consultation, local consensus | Mandated execution |
| Adoption dynamics | Dual-speed, hybrid channels | High digital enforcement |
| Economic driver | FDI-driven data-centre supply chain | State-directed industrial tech |

1. **Execution speed.** China's top-down directives allow rapid rollout of unified platforms (City Brain;
   一网统管 "one network unified management"). Johor needs alignment from the federal Digital Ministry through the
   state to 16 councils.
2. **Data governance.** China enforces centralisation through municipal big-data bureaus. Johor has data silos
   across federal (JPJ, LHDN), state land office (PTG) and municipal databases, so a citizen super-app needs
   explicit inter-governmental agreements.
3. **Inclusivity.** China relies on mandatory digital ID and the WeChat/Alipay mini-program ecosystem. Johor needs
   physical-digital hybrid touchpoints such as PEDi (*Pusat Ekonomi Digital*) centres.
4. **Talent.** Chinese cities use in-house tech units, SOEs and local tech giants. Johor relies on private vendors
   and federal/state IT agencies, and competes for engineers with data-centre operators and Singapore.

## 6. Web-sourced additions and corrections (2026-10-17)

- **Data-centre scale.** As of Q2 2025, Johor had approved 42 data-centre projects worth RM164.45 bn. The state
  says they will create 6,000+ jobs and represent 78.6% of Malaysia's operational IT capacity. The state is now
  more selective, favouring green tech, and has turned some proposals down on environmental and water grounds.
  ([The Sun](https://thesun.my/news/malaysia-news/johor-approves-42-data-centre-projects-in-q22025-lg14569174/),
  [Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2025/07/29/johors-digital-economy-gets-boost-with-42-approved-data-centre-projects-in-q2-2025-says-state-exco/185669),
  [AsiaNews](https://www.asianews.it/en/south-east-asia/malaysia/johor-rejects-some-requests-for-new-data-centres-to-protect-the-environment))
- **Data-centre strategy phases.** Phase 1 (DC build-out) is due by 2027. Phase 2 covers the supporting ecosystem
  (cooling, racks, PCBs). Phase 3 covers AI, cloud and big data.
  ([MIDA](https://www.mida.gov.my/mida-news/first-phase-of-johors-data-centre-strategic-plan-set-for-completion-by-2027-says-exco-man/))
  The planning guidelines are published:
  [JPBD Johor PDF](https://jpbd.johor.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/81-GP-DATA-CENTRE-en.pdf).
- **Johor Digital Plan.** A Johor Digital Council and a Digital Master Plan to 2030 were announced in 2020,
  covering government transformation, industry and community. The earlier vehicle, Digital Johor Sdn Bhd (2017),
  is widely seen as having failed. The plan was still referenced in 2025, but its current published form is
  unclear. ([Bernama](https://www.bernama.com/en/business/news.php?id=1905606),
  [Digital News Asia](https://digitalnewsasia.com/insights/can-tmj-seize-moment-and-be-dynamic-leader-johors-digital-ambitions-need))
- **Johor–Singapore SEZ and IMFC-J.** The SEZ agreement was signed on 7 Jan 2025. The Invest Malaysia
  Facilitation Centre – Johor (IMFC-J) is a joint federal-state one-stop centre (IRDA, Invest Johor, MIDA) that
  coordinates investment applications and approvals end to end. It self-reported 1,000 enquiries and RM73 bn of
  potential investment as of March 2026.
  ([Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/29749/and-malaysia-exchange-agreement-on-the-johor-singapore-special-economic-zone),
  [Malay Mail press release](https://www.malaymail.com/news/money/mediaoutreach/2026/07/02/forest-city-sfz-highlights-early-js-sez-traction-as-investment-pipeline-expands/474217))
- **Data Sharing Act 2025 (Act 864).** Royal Assent on 5 Feb 2025. It governs data sharing **between federal
  public-sector bodies only**: it does **not** cover state government agencies or private parties. Any data
  flow between Johor and federal bodies (PADU, MyDigital ID, JPJ, LHDN) therefore needs its own arrangement.
  ([Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/malaysia-enacts-data-sharing-rules-for-public-sector),
  [DFDL](https://www.dfdl.com/insights/legal-and-tax-updates/data-sharing-act-2025/),
  [IIUM paper](https://journals.iium.edu.my/kict/index.php/jisdt/article/view/693))
- **Building permits (OSC 3.0 Plus).** This is the federal online one-stop-centre system rolled out to all 89
  Peninsular local authorities, including Johor's. In 2023 the government announced the approval target would drop
  from 42 to 21 days and referral agencies from 19 to 5. We have no Johor-specific actuals yet.
  ([The Vibes](https://www.thevibes.com/index.php/articles/news/90651/govt-reduces-osc-3.0-plus-approval-period-to-21-days),
  [World Bank e-permitting](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/705331592344507733/pdf/From-Paper-to-the-Cloud-Improving-Building-Control-through-E-permitting.pdf))
- **Gap: the Johor data lake itself.** We found no public information on the state data lake's scope, owner,
  datasets or platform. This is the first thing to get from the client.
