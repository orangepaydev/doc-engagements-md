## Idea
The Johor State Government is building a data lake and wants to know what to do with it next. We benchmark it
against the digital-government path of Chinese cities and provinces (Zhejiang/Hangzhou, Shanghai, Shenzhen,
Suzhou, Beijing). They went in roughly this order: cut paperwork and visits first, then share data and
e-certificates across departments, then automate approvals, then run city operations from one platform, and
finally open up public data to the market. Our aim is a shortlist of projects Johor could launch on top of the data
lake, sorted three ways:

- **Low-hanging fruit**: quick wins that cut paperwork and processing time.
- **Infrastructure / enablers**: shared foundations that make later automation and services possible.
- **Strategic long-term**: new services that attract investment or make life better for local businesses and
  citizens.

Source: client engagement (Johor government data lake). Why now: the data-centre boom and the Johor–Singapore SEZ
(signed Jan 2025) are putting pressure on approvals and investor facilitation, and the data lake gives Johor a data
foundation that most of the Chinese reforms needed first.

Big caveat to carry into Validation: China's model works through top-down mandates and enforced data
centralisation. Johor works under federal / state / 16 local-authority (PBT) governance with consensus-based
rollouts. Malaysia's Data Sharing Act 2025 covers federal agencies only, not state agencies. And Johor has a
two-speed citizen base that needs physical counters kept open. So we borrow the *sequence and mechanisms* from
China, not its enforcement model.
