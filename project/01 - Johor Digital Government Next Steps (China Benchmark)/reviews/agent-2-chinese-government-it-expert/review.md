# Expert Review — Chinese Government IT Delivery Perspective

- Reviewer: agent briefed as a senior expert with deep experience bidding for and delivering IT/digital-government
  projects in China (vendor/integrator ecosystem serving municipal governments; 一网通办, 一网统管, 秒批,
  高效办成一件事, City Brain, 数据局/大数据集团 institutional models). Four targeted claims were verified against
  Chinese-language sources.
- Date: 2026-10-17
- Reviewed: all five documents in this project folder
- Question asked: are the assumptions and opportunities realistic? Honest opinion requested.
- Caveat: agent-generated review; claims marked with sources below were web-verified, the rest are professional
  judgement and should be treated as a practitioner's opinion.

---

## (1) Overall verdict

These are unusually honest research notes by deck standards — the self-reporting caveats, the KL City Brain
dead-end, and the DSA-2025 federal-only gap are all correct and well spotted. But the China story is still a
curated success reel with failure modes footnoted rather than load-bearing: several marquee precedents quietly
assume coercion, scale or SOE financing Johor lacks, at least three headline numbers are wrong or internally
inconsistent, and the recommended owner/operator model imports 管运分离 at the precise moment China itself is
retreating from it.

## (2) Accuracy of the China narrative

**Right:** the 最多跑一次 timeline and 2018 provincial codification; 秒批's design including SMS/manual fallback;
高效办成一件事 as 国发〔2024〕3号 with a 13-item first batch; Henan's 1,317 red-coded depositors and 5 disciplined
officials; the 86% Weibo backlash against Hangzhou's permanent health code; and above all the "no post-pilot
evaluation of KL City Brain" claim — my search confirms nothing exists beyond launch coverage and a 2020 ITU piece
still speaking in "expected outcomes."

**Wrong or overclaimed:**
- *"100% of administrative procedures could be handled online by Jan 2019"* — the official claim was coverage of
  items within the reform's scope (覆盖率), not end-to-end online completion; follow-up reporting found items still
  requiring offline materials. As written, this will be repeated to the client as fact.
- **Suzhou 信易贷 "500bn yuan+ to 8,600+ firms"** — arithmetically absurd (~58m yuan per firm). Almost certainly a
  national-platform figure or a unit error. The register flags it; the case-study headline still repeats it.
  Unfixable credibility damage if a client spots it first.
- **Beijing Data Group**: the text says 60bn yuan capital, the register says 6bn — both muddled. Verified:
  registered July 2025 at 3bn, raised to 6bn in Jan 2026. It is months old; citing it as a proven model is
  premature.
- **Shanghai Data Exchange is presented as the working pole vs Guiyang the failure.** Actual volumes: ~100m yuan
  (2022), ~1.1bn (2023), ~4bn+ (2024). Still tiny, and growth is policy-manufactured (data assets on SOE balance
  sheets). Shanghai is Guiyang with a policy tailwind, not proof of a functioning market. C6's implied endpoint is
  weaker than the doc suggests.
- **Digital Guangdong is framed as "Tencent + telcos; why it worked: one accountable operator."** In Dec 2021
  China Electronics took 62.84% (32.92% CEC + 29.92% CEC Cloud) and Tencent was diluted from 49% to ~16.7% — at a
  pre-money valuation of just 415m yuan. The celebrated JV became a central-SOE subsidiary at a modest valuation.
  That drift, buried in register row 15, is the *real* lesson about operator models, and it cuts against the doc's
  own M2 enthusiasm.
- **"The order matters"** is cleaner than history: Shanghai built 一网通办 and 一网统管 in parallel from 2018–19;
  Zhejiang built data sharing *concurrently* with 最多跑一次. Fine as pedagogy, misleading as a dependency claim.

## (3) What transfers vs. what assumes Chinese preconditions

**Genuinely transferable:** A1, A4, A5, B1 (correctly critical-path), B3, B6, B5 (the most Johor-native item —
DuitNow/FPX rails exist; the gap is reconciliation and unified receipts, not payments), and C5 — water is a state
matter via Ranhill SAJ, so this is a real state lever, though its cited precedent ("City Brain resource
monitoring") is bogus; the honest precedent is domestic utility regulation.

**Quietly assume coercion/scale:**
- **C1 秒批**: Shenzhen's ~200 items leaned on e-certificates with national legal equivalence, real-name ID
  infrastructure, and vertical agencies under municipal pressure — and were data-verifiable items (registrations,
  subsidies). Johor's municipal approvals are physical-compliance matters; the auto-approvable slice is
  renewals-only, far thinner than "200 items" implies. "No human in the loop" also carries a judicial-review /
  natural-justice exposure Chinese administrative law doesn't pose equivalently.
- **C2 一件事** worked because the State Council could order vertical agencies (tax, social security, utilities).
  "Move to Johor" is mostly *federal* items (NRD, immigration, TNB). The state can bundle only its own thin layer.
- **C4 City Brain/一网统管**: Hangzhou had one traffic-police authority over 1,300 signals plus a hometown-giant
  subsidy logic. Johor's roads are split across 16 PBTs, federal JKR roads and police. The docs correctly cite
  KL's failure but still proceed to C4.
- **C3 信易贷** ran on state-directed bank lending quotas plus national tax data. Malaysian banks are BNM-regulated
  and profit-driven; CCRIS/CTOS already exist; marginal value of assessment/utility data is unproven.

Notably, the docs **underuse Johor's one real coercion channel**: the state appoints council presidents and
controls grants — the functional Malaysian equivalent of 干部考核. That substitution should be explicit throughout.

## (4) Failure lessons understated

1. **The smart-city vendor receivables crisis.** The Guizhou county case (from one obscure source,
   newpolis.media) is one instance of a systemic 2022–24 pattern of governments not paying smart-city vendors,
   with several integrators going under. For a longlist proposing an operator and PBT-consumed shared services,
   "who pays, and will they pay" is China's #1 failure lesson; here it's an anecdote. 独山县-type cases aren't
   cited at all.
2. **大屏 formalism.** 一网统管's own reputational problem — command centers that impress delegations but change
   nothing operationally — is the exact failure mode awaiting Mersing and Kota Tinggi, whose binding constraint is
   assessment collection, not dashboards. Absent.
3. **Institutional churn.** The 数据局 model being recommended is mid-restructure (National Data Bureau 2023;
   local bureau mergers). Sound advice for Johor, but don't sell it as "the settled Chinese model."
4. **Official-side burden.** 浙政钉's 715 apps for 1.23m officials shows internal-tool sprawl rots these programs;
   A5 assumes counter staff will absorb it.
5. **Crisis-app mortality**: health codes were stood down wholesale; single-purpose platforms rarely survive their
   crisis — relevant to C2's sustainability. Also Beijing 12345's 97% resolution rate is self-defined; metric
   gaming is documented.

## (5) Changes to the longlist before it faces the client

1. **Fix the numbers**: Suzhou 500bn, Beijing Data Group 60bn/6bn, reframe "100% online" as item-coverage; recast
   Shanghai Data Exchange as a second slow-start case.
2. **Rescope C1** to renewals-only with an explicit legal-equivalence precondition (Electronic Commerce Act 2006 /
   E-Government Activities Act 2007 + state enactments) and judicial-review-safe fallback design.
3. **Demote C4** from state-wide platform to a priced MBJB/MBIP pilot with a named payer — or cut; make the
   unresolved KL City Brain question a *gate*, not a footnote.
4. **Add a "who pays / product owner" column** to every row. For the operator model, use Digital Guangdong's
   ownership drift and thin valuation as the cautionary version of 管运分离; and correct the conflation — the
   5-year term limit comes from public-data *authorised-operation* rules, not platform-JV practice (Digital
   Guangdong is 8+ years and counting).
5. **Flag OrangePay's interest** on B5/C3 — "Fits OrangePay's payments and fintech position" reads as vendor
   capture to any client reviewer.
6. **Make the grant-conditionality/appointment lever explicit** as Johor's substitute for Chinese mandates — the
   docs' best unexploited insight.
7. **Rescope C2** to state-only bundles; mark federal-touching bundles as MoU-dependent multi-year items.
